## PRIORITY 1 — IMMEDIATE SAFETY SITUATIONS

### 1. Smoke is coming from the van
1. **Short answer:** If it feels dangerous, call 911 first. Either way, call your BC to inform them.
2. **Steps:** (1) Assess danger — if dangerous, call 911. (2) Call your BC and tell them what happened.
3. **Conditions:** Applies any time smoke is noticed coming from the vehicle, regardless of source.
4. **Exceptions:** None specified.
5. **Contacts:** 911 (if dangerous), BC (always).
6. **Smallest clarification:** None needed — answer applies regardless of visible fire vs. smoke-only.

### 2. Driver feels sick or dizzy while driving
1. **Short answer:** Stop in a safe location, then call your BC.
2. **Steps:** (1) Pull over to a safe location. (2) Call your BC.
3. **Conditions:** Any time the driver feels unsafe to continue driving due to illness/dizziness.
4. **Exceptions:** None specified.
5. **Contacts:** BC.
6. **Smallest clarification:** None needed.

### 3. Downed power lines near a delivery
1. **Short answer:** Find and notify the customer, then call your BC and pass along the customer's info.
2. **Steps:** (1) Locate the customer and tell them about the power lines. (2) Call your BC, explain what happened, and provide the customer's contact info.
3. **Conditions:** Applies whenever downed power lines are found near a delivery location.
4. **Exceptions:** None specified — whether the delivery is completed is not dictated by this procedure.
5. **Contacts:** Customer, then BC.
6. **Smallest clarification:** None needed.

### 4. Flat tire on route
1. **Short answer:** Same as the standard vehicle-breakdown procedure — no separate procedure for a flat tire specifically.
2. **Steps:** (1) Turn on hazard lights. (2) Place warning triangles per DOT standard (within 10 minutes of stopping) — one ~10 ft behind, one ~100 ft behind, one ~100 ft ahead on a two-way road (adjust placement for one-way/divided roads or blocked visibility). (3) Call your BC.
3. **Conditions:** Any flat tire on route.
4. **Exceptions:** None specified.
5. **Contacts:** BC.
6. **Smallest clarification:** None needed.

## PRIORITY 2 — VEHICLE, SCANNER, AND APP FAILURES

### 5. Keys locked in the van
1. **Short answer:** Call your BC — they may know where a spare key is.
2. **Steps:** (1) Call BC.
3. **Conditions:** Driver is locked out of the van.
4. **Exceptions:** No self-help entry method specified — do not attempt to force entry.
5. **Contacts:** BC.
6. **Smallest clarification:** None needed.

### 6. Scanner freezes
1. **Short answer:** Restart the scanner; if that fails, remove and reinsert the battery; if still not working, notify your BC for a replacement scanner and use hand sheets in the meantime.
2. **Steps:** (1) Restart the scanner. (2) If unresolved, remove and reinsert the battery. (3) If still not working, notify BC so they can bring a replacement scanner. (4) While waiting, record deliveries on hand sheets (blue sheets).
3. **Conditions:** Scanner is powered on but unresponsive — distinct from low battery (separate existing procedure) and a completely dead device (separate existing procedure, hand sheet only).
4. **Exceptions:** None specified.
5. **Contacts:** BC (if steps 1–2 don't resolve it).
6. **Smallest clarification:** Confirm the scanner is powered on but frozen, not simply out of battery or completely non-functional — those have their own separate procedures.

### 7. FORGE app crashes during a delivery
1. **Short answer:** Restart the scanner; if that doesn't help, check with your BC (possible maintenance); keep delivering using hand sheets in the meantime.
2. **Steps:** (1) Restart the scanner. (2) If that doesn't resolve it, contact BC — there may be maintenance underway. (3) Continue deliveries using hand sheets (blue sheets). (4) Turn in the hand sheets at the station office at the end of the day.
3. **Conditions:** The FORGE app itself crashes or becomes unusable mid-delivery.
4. **Exceptions:** None specified.
5. **Contacts:** BC.
6. **Smallest clarification:** None needed. **Note:** "blue sheet" and "hand sheet" refer to the same physical document — should be treated as equivalent terms throughout ReadyRoute, same as "Vision Label" / "SID sticker."

### 8. Missing vehicle barcode, vehicle number already known
1. **Short answer:** Yes, the driver can proceed directly — no need to re-ask for the vehicle number if it was already supplied.
2. **Steps:** (1) Use any reputable Code 128 barcode generator. (2) Enter the vehicle number with a capital "V" prefix (e.g., V538765). (3) Generate the barcode. (4) Scan it directly off the screen.
3. **Conditions:** Vehicle number is already known/supplied; barcode generator is set to Code 128.
4. **Exceptions:** None specified.
5. **Contacts:** None required.
6. **Smallest clarification:** ReadyRoute should not re-ask for the vehicle number if the driver already provided it in the original question.

## PRIORITY 3 — DELIVERY, RECIPIENT, AND CUSTOMER SITUATIONS

### 9. Children answer the door without an adult
1. **Short answer:** A minor cannot sign for any signature-required package. If the package doesn't require a signature, it can be left with a photo taken.
2. **Steps:** (1) Confirm whether the package requires a signature (ASR/DSR/ISR/alcohol/tobacco/etc). (2) If it does, a minor (under 18) cannot sign — treat as the standard "no eligible signer" outcome for that signature type (retain for reattempt, or apply the applicable code). (3) If no signature is required, leave the package (driver release) and take a PPOD photo.
3. **Conditions:** Only a minor is present, no adult.
4. **Exceptions:** No special handling beyond the standard no-eligible-signer procedure for the applicable package type.
5. **Contacts:** None required unless the underlying signature-type procedure calls for one.
6. **Smallest clarification:** Does the package require a signature, and if so, which type (ASR/DSR/ISR)?

### 10. Customer disputes a completed delivery
1. **Short answer:** Notify your BC — they can track the package using internal systems.
2. **Steps:** (1) Contact BC and inform them of the dispute.
3. **Conditions:** Customer states they didn't receive a package the driver marked as delivered.
4. **Exceptions:** Driver does not independently investigate or return to the address without direction.
5. **Contacts:** BC.
6. **Smallest clarification:** None needed.

### 11. Customer asks for release to a nearby stranger
1. **Short answer:** Not permitted unless the person is a genuine neighbor and the customer gives permission by phone. Otherwise, treat as a non-delivery.
2. **Steps:** (1) Confirm the person is a neighbor. (2) Call the customer to confirm permission. (3) If confirmed, release may proceed as an indirect delivery. (4) If not — apply code 007, cross the package, remove the Vision Label (SID sticker), and return it to the station.
3. **Conditions:** Customer requests release to someone other than themselves who isn't already covered by an existing indirect-delivery/ISR process.
4. **Exceptions:** Must be a neighbor with confirmed customer permission — a stranger with no relationship to the customer is never eligible.
5. **Contacts:** Customer (for permission).
6. **Smallest clarification:** Is the requested person a neighbor, and has the customer given permission?

### 12. Customer records the driver on camera
1. **Short answer:** Being recorded is fine — it's the customer's property and their right. If it turns into confrontation or harassment, that's a separate hostile-customer situation.
2. **Steps:** (1) If it's an ordinary camera (doorbell, security, etc.) — no action needed. (2) If it escalates into confrontation or harassment — treat as a personal-security situation: protect yourself, don't escalate, leave if needed, report to BC.
3. **Conditions:** Distinguishes ordinary recording from active confrontation/harassment.
4. **Exceptions:** None specified for media or on-FedEx-premises recording specifically — treated the same as ordinary recording unless it becomes confrontational.
5. **Contacts:** BC (only if it escalates to a hostile situation).
6. **Smallest clarification:** Is the situation just being recorded, or is there active confrontation/harassment occurring?

### 13. Customer calls or texts to request a changed delivery address
1. **Short answer:** The driver only follows what the shipping label says. A customer can't authorize an address change by contacting the driver directly — they must call FedEx.
2. **Steps:** (1) Do not change the delivery address based on a direct customer call/text. (2) Continue using the label's address. (3) If the customer states they've moved from that address, apply code 002.
3. **Conditions:** Customer attempts to redirect delivery via direct contact with the driver.
4. **Exceptions:** Driver must never self-authorize an address change from a direct customer request.
5. **Contacts:** None required from the driver — the customer must contact FedEx directly.
6. **Smallest clarification:** Has the customer moved, or are they just requesting delivery somewhere else?

## PRIORITY 4 — DUPLICATE PACKAGES AND TRACKING NUMBERS

### 14. Two physical packages with the same tracking number
1. **Short answer:** Deliver both packages. Only one can be scanned (shared barcode); note in comments that duplicate packages were delivered.
2. **Steps:** (1) Confirm both packages share the identical tracking number/barcode. (2) Scan one (the barcode is identical, so only one scan is possible). (3) Deliver both physical packages. (4) Add a comment noting that two (or more) duplicate packages were delivered under that tracking number.
3. **Conditions:** Two physical packages share an identical tracking number/barcode.
4. **Exceptions:** None specified — both are delivered without waiting for management resolution.
5. **Contacts:** None required.
6. **Smallest clarification:** Confirm the packages genuinely share the same tracking number (not just a similar one) before applying this procedure.

### 15. Two apparent duplicate packages for one customer
1. **Short answer:** Same as above — deliver both, scan one, note the duplicate in comments.
2. **Steps:** Same as item 14.
3. **Conditions:** Same as item 14 — this situation and item 14 resolve to the same procedure.
4. **Exceptions:** Same as item 14.
5. **Contacts:** None required.
6. **Smallest clarification:** Confirm whether the two packages share the same tracking number (this procedure) or are genuinely separate orders with different tracking numbers (deliver both normally, no special handling needed).

## PRIORITY 6 — DIRECT TERMINOLOGY DEFINITIONS

### 17. DNA
**Definition:** DNA = **Did Not Attempt**, corresponds to **Code 027** ("Package Not Delivered - No Attempt"). It's a system status code, not something the driver types out as an abbreviation.
**Immediate action required on seeing it:** No — it's a status label, not an action trigger by itself.

### 18. OP-201
**Definition:** OP-201 is a **signed authorization on file** at a business, allowing driver release of non-signature-required packages without a new signature each time. **Important:** even with OP-201 on file, the driver must still confirm with the customer and call CXPC before releasing — it is not automatic.
**Immediate action required on seeing it:** Yes — confirm with the customer and call CXPC before proceeding, even though OP-201 exists.

### 19. Call tag
**Definition:** A call tag is a pickup request for a package the customer is returning — FedEx sends the driver to pick it up from the customer's location, similar to a delivery stop.
**Mechanic:** When retrieving the package, the driver puts the call tag label on it, scans it, and applies the "call tag picked up" code.
**Immediate action required on seeing it:** Yes, when handling one — see mechanic above.

### 20. OSA
**Definition:** UNKNOWN / NEEDS ANOTHER REVIEW. Confirmed to appear as "030 — Retail Refusal/O.S.A." in MGB-119, but the exact expansion of the abbreviation is not yet confirmed.

### 21. BC
**Definition:** BC = **Business Contact** — the driver's manager/contact for reporting issues, getting direction, and escalating field situations.
**Immediate action required on seeing it:** No.

### 22. FORGE
**Definition:** FORGE is FedEx's internal system for delivery and pickup operations — it runs on the scanner and drives the day-to-day route workflow.
**Immediate action required on seeing it:** No.

### 23. Service cross
**Definition:** A service cross is the notation written on a package that doesn't get delivered, in a specific cross-shaped format with four quadrants: top-left = status code (with name/initial), top-right = time, bottom-left = date, bottom-right = Work Area #.
**Where the driver sees/uses it:** Written directly on the package before returning it to the station.
**Immediate action required on seeing it:** Yes — this is an action the driver performs, not just a term they encounter.

### 24. Manifest
**Definition:** A manifest is the digital list of packages assigned to the driver's route in FORGE. It doesn't always match the physical van contents — a package handler can scan a package to the wrong Work Area, meaning something can be "on the manifest" without being physically present, or vice versa.
**Immediate action required on seeing it:** No, unless a manifest/physical mismatch is found, which is its own separate procedure.

### 25. CXPC
**Definition:** CXPC is the contact for customer-facing coordination, distinct from BC (which is for internal/operational support). A driver calls CXPC when: they can't reach the customer for a delivery, they need help resolving an incomplete/missing address, or for any pickup issue (transferring a pickup, reaching a business customer, extending a pickup window).
**Immediate action required on seeing it:** No — it's a contact resource, used as needed per the situations above.
**Note:** Exact letter-by-letter expansion of "CXPC" not yet confirmed — the functional definition above is complete for driver purposes.
