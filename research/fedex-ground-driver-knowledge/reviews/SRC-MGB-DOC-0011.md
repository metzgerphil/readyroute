## Page map

- Page 1: cover and source identity.
- Page 2: prevalence and prevention context; any dog can bite, including one that initially appears friendly.
- Page 3: avoid an unfamiliar dog if possible; do not assume health or ease from appearance; a dog's disposition can be misread.
- Page 4: complete approaching-dog sequence—stay still/calm, avoid direct eye contact, do not panic/make loud noises/run, use a firm deep voice, turn one side toward the dog, protect the neck, wait or slowly back away.
- Page 5: knockdown response—curl into a ball, tuck the head, protect ears/neck, and consider an object barrier including a package if necessary; breed does not determine aggression, bite strength, or risk.
- Page 6: wound response—request proof of rabies vaccination when the owner is present; record owner/veterinarian/contact information; wash hands and clean the wound with soap and water; consult a doctor immediately or use the ER after office hours; contact the veterinarian; report the bite to local animal control.
- Page 7: business discussion prompt and related-material routing; no additional operational procedure.
