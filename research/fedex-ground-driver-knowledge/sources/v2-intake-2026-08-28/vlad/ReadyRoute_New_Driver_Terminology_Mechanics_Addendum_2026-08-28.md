# ReadyRoute — New-Driver Terminology & Mechanics: Addendum Report

**Date:** August 28, 2026  
**Source:** Live testing on the updated RRA Test Console, plus questions gathered directly from drivers by Vlad.

## Standing principle

Every answer is written for a new driver who is already overwhelmed. Use concrete, tap-level mechanics wherever possible, not only the conceptual code or outcome.

“Vision Label” and “SID sticker” refer to the same physical label and must be recognized as equivalent terminology.

## Approved findings and supplied answers

### Tire tread depth

Add the DOT-sourced minimums to the unified vehicle-inspection answer: front/steering tires require 4/32 inch and other tires require 2/32 inch under 49 CFR 393.75. Include the penny test with an appropriate explanation of its limit.

### Same-day misdelivery recovery mechanics

1. Scan the package again to confirm the misdelivery and apply Code 17.
2. Go to the correct address.
3. Scan the package again for delivery.
4. Verify the address when the scanner asks, then tap confirm.
5. Process the delivery as usual.
6. If the same-day redelivery succeeds, the stop closes as Code 18.

### WA

WA means Work Area, another word for the route. Find it as the first three or four numbers on the Vision Label/SID sticker.

### Signature capture

When a signature is needed, or the driver selects Met Customer even without a required signature, a signature box appears automatically after the driver taps the check mark. Have the customer sign in the box, then enter the customer's first initial and full last name.

### Cash on delivery

Have the customer call the driver's BC. No fuller COD procedure is approved by this addendum.

### DSR compared with ISR

DSR requires an in-person signature at the labeled delivery address and does not allow a neighbor at another address to sign. ISR can allow an eligible neighbor's signature with the required indirect-delivery and door-tag documentation. A conceptual “what is the difference” question should receive this explanation rather than the operational selector flow.

### Torn label with a scannable barcode

If the barcode still scans and the package itself is not damaged, deliver it normally. Check package condition first. If the package is damaged, use the damaged-package procedure.

### Lunch break and logging

Ask the BC because break policy and the practical logging process can vary. Keep this separate from a question specifically asking about federal HOS break requirements.

### Hazmat label terminology

A diamond-shaped hazardous-material label on a package identifies the type of hazard, such as flammable, corrosive, or toxic, and may be accompanied by an identification number. Treat it as hazmat: verify the required paperwork, such as the applicable manifest or SF-136, and follow the relevant hazmat procedure rather than treating it as an ordinary package.

### Accidental delivered scan before finalization

If the driver catches the mistake immediately before it finalizes, cancel it, return to the main menu, and scan the correct package. This is separate from using Delete Scan on Stop Details for an already-committed wrong-package scan and separate from recovering a completed misdelivery.

## Confirmed working examples

- Wrong package scanned into the stop: tap and hold the package on Stop Details, select Delete Scan from Package Action, and confirm the package check mark disappears.
- PPOD: show the package at the actual release location with useful surroundings, review for clarity, and tap DONE; do not use a distant, blurry, vehicle, or people-focused image.
